# war3d

A work-in-progress demo of an interactive 3D map of the Chinese People's War of Resistance Against Japan.

This project is being made to commemorate the 81st anniversary of the Chinese People's Victory in the War of Resistance Against Japan (1945–2026).

**Status: early development.** Only the First Battle of Changsha (September–October 1939) is implemented so far — it serves as a testbed for the terrain, rendering and data pipeline. The eventual goal is a continuous 1931–1945 timeline.

## What works today

### Terrain and water

- Real terrain from AWS Terrain Tiles: a 1024² float32 DEM covering roughly 487 × 333 km of northern Hunan, Hubei and Jiangxi (111–116°E, 27.4–30.4°N), exaggerated 8× vertically so ridgelines read at this scale
- Satellite imagery (ESRI World_Imagery) is used only to modulate the brightness of a fixed campaign-map palette, so the relief keeps its texture without turning grey
- Rivers, Dongting Lake and the Xiangjiang take their outlines from the ESRI World_Topo_Map water layer (2048² mask) instead of hand-drawn polygons; a distance-to-shore field drives the depth shading
- The water level is solved automatically from the mask and the DEM is carved underneath it, so rivers sit inside their valleys rather than on top of the ground
- Water shading: depth tint, Fresnel sky reflection, wind-driven brightness and sun glints; river and lake surfaces form one continuous water system

### Timeline

- 1939-09-01 → 1939-10-31 on a continuous (fractional-day) clock: play / pause, speed slider, ← / → to step a day, Home to return to the start of the battle
- 34 sourced events, highlighted in the side panel as they become active
- 22 narration segments that cover every day of the window — order of battle, the "withdraw and decide" plan, the crossing of the Xinqiang and Miluo rivers, the supply-line overstretch, the Japanese withdrawal order and the pursuit

### Military-map symbols

- Attack axes drawn as arrows that grow along the timeline; the arrowhead pulses and a slow brightness wave runs down the shaft while an axis is still moving
- Retreats drawn as flowing 2 km dashes (split into 0.25 km sub-segments so they slide smoothly); completed arrows fade to a faint trace of the campaign instead of vanishing on a single frame
- Defence lines as continuous lines with hachures that breathe with time
- Unit boxes (rectangle + cross, the infantry symbol) at the departure point of each attack / pursuit axis, de-duplicated so neighbouring units do not stack
- Battle-site crosses at engagements, pulsing while the fight is active
- Occupied-area shading that fades in and out with the event dates

### Labels and interaction

- Place names, event names while they are active, and unit names that follow the current head of each arrow
- Screen-space label collision avoidance: overlapping labels shift upward, then drop out for that frame
- Orbit / pan / zoom, three preset views (overview, northern Hunan, Changsha), click any point to fly the camera there; zoom is clamped to 65 km so close-ups never expose the limits of the data
- The camera can be moved at any time without interrupting playback

## Build

Windows, MSVC + Ninja. All dependencies are vendored under `third_party/`; no external paths are used.

```powershell
powershell -NoProfile -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File build.ps1
```

Output is `build\war3d.exe`. OpenGL 4.3 required.

Optional debug flags:

```powershell
build\war3d.exe --bench                                  # offscreen frame-time benchmark
build\war3d.exe --shot out.bmp --date 19390926 --near    # fixed date, close camera, save a BMP
```

## Data

Historical events, sources, movements, defensive lines and narration live in `data/*.csv`. Every event carries a source ID and a confidence level, and the conflicting Chinese and Japanese casualty figures are listed side by side rather than merged into one number.

The raster layers are generated by the scripts in `tools/` (Python + Pillow, AWS Terrarium / ESRI tiles cached under `tools/.topo_cache/`):

- `build_dem.py` — downloads DEM tiles and writes `terrain_hunan_float32.raw`
- `build_albedo.py` — downloads imagery and writes `terrain_albedo_rgb.raw`
- `build_water_mask.py` — extracts the water layer, writes `water_mask_u8.raw` and `water_depth_u8.raw`

Three caveats worth knowing:

- River courses and the water mask come from map tiles, not surveyed geometry. Shapes are close, but not authoritative, and small rivers are slightly widened by the mask cleanup.
- Occupied areas and defensive lines are schematic, traced from textual descriptions in the sources.
- Movement paths connect verified place-name coordinates; they show axes, not exact unit tracks.

This is a demo, not an academic reference.
